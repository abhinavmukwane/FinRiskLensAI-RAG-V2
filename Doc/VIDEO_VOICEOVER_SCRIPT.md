## Recording notes

- **Pace: 154 words per minute average.** 571 words against 222 seconds, and no block
  above 200. Normal presentation pace is 140–160, so this is a comfortable read with room
  to breathe. Screen recordings need air, and the numbers need a beat to land.
- **Every `‖` is a hard pause** (0.5–1 s). Do not read through them.
- **Say numbers the way a banker says them:** "six hundred and eighty", "six point one
  five crore", "ninety out of a hundred".
- **Do not speed up to catch a cue.** If you run late, drop a whole sentence instead —
  the three blocks worth trimming are named under Word budget.
- Record dry, one block at a time, and align each block's start to its timecode. Blocks
  are written to be independently re-recordable.
- Accent/pronunciation: **Udyam** = "ood-yum", **Finvu** = "fin-voo", **beckn** =
  "beacon", **OCEN** = "oh-sen", **ULI** = spell it out, "you-el-eye".

## What is deliberately not said

- **No claim that the three sources agree.** The cross-source panel on screen reads GST
  ₹2.99 Cr/month, ITR ₹2.95 L/month and bank credits ₹29,500/month. Those are three
  different orders of magnitude, because the linked sandbox account carries almost none
  of the real turnover. The narration therefore never says the sources corroborate each
  other — it points at the **weak** transaction dimension instead, which is both true and
  more convincing. **If a judge asks about that panel**, the honest answer is that the
  linked account is a sandbox account with a fraction of the business's flows, which is
  exactly why the model scored that dimension 69 out of 150.
- **No "approved", no "sanctioned".** The script says *indicative* and *the bank decides*.
- **No claim of a live LOS/ULI/ONDC connection.** The line is "recorded, not sent, and the
  screen says so" — which matches the `(sim)` badge visible in the same frame.
- **No named person.** Director names are on screen for two seconds; the narration does
  not read them out.
